The opportunity in one paragraph

Hamid runs two businesses: Eastern Pharmaceutical Group and Apollo Nutraceuticals. Eastern is at capacity this year (enough orders and contracts, no room for new markets — revisit around 2028). Apollo is the live opportunity: a B2B nutraceuticals manufacturer that wants to expand into new international markets now. He has named the target markets himself: NZ, UAE, Vietnam, Singapore, Korea and Japan. This is exactly the multi-market B2B entry work Human Digital does.

Why this is a strong lead: he named his own target markets, confirmed B2B is the main business, has a clear expansion intent, and pulled a partner (Michael) into the meeting. He is engaged and serious — but he is a careful, proof-first buyer. Treat this as a credibility and scoping conversation, not a pitch to close.

Landmines — read these before the call

1. No retainers. This is a hard no.
"We don't do any retainer business at all… we've had bad experiences multiple times with different groups. That's out of the table for us." Do not raise retainer. We've already agreed to project-scoped, phased work (strategy and market prioritisation first, then execution per market as separate scopes). Frame everything that way.
2. He buys on proof, not promises.
He twice pushed back asking who we are and what our expertise is before agreeing to meet ("tell me about yourself before a meeting please"). Lead with credibility and track record, not process. He explicitly asked for "a proper walk-through of Human Digital and your track record on Australian brand business development" — that is the core ask for this call.
3. B2B fit was flagged honestly — keep it that way.
We proactively flagged that if Apollo were D2C-led, a consumer agency would suit them better. He confirmed B2B is the main business (D2C is small). That honesty landed well and built trust — stay straight and evidence-led, he responds to candour.

What we committed to bring

  • A proper walk-through of Human Digital — who we are, what we do.
  • Track record on business development for Australian brands, with concrete outcomes.
  • How we'd approach Apollo's specific target markets.

Suggested prep and talking points

  • Lead with the most B2B, market-entry-relevant proof. NZ Pump (legacy brand → global authority) is the strongest story here; support with Alliance Woodware and Washtech (AU/NZ manufacturers into new markets). Mention ABB and Zeiss as international brands we've expanded into AUNZ, it shows we work both directions.
  • Show a point of view on his six markets. A quick, credible read on buyer profile and channel per market (NZ, UAE, Vietnam, Singapore, Korea, Japan) will land hard — it proves expertise before any scope discussion.
  • Present the engagement as phases, not a retainer. Phase 1: strategy and market prioritisation (which of the six to lead with, buyer profile, positioning). Phase 2 onward: execution per market as separate project scopes, with people on the ground shaping the work rather than translating from AU.
  • Have Michael in mind. He's a decision-maker too — make sure the walk-through speaks to both.

Questions to get answered on the call

  • Which of the six markets is priority one, and why? (Timeline pressure on any of them?)
  • What does a successful first 6–12 months look like to them?
  • Budget / investment appetite for a phased engagement.
  • What did the "bad retainer experiences" look like — so we design the engagement to avoid exactly that.
  • Decision process between Hamid and Michael.

How to play it

He's analytical, direct, and values honesty and expertise over polish. Win the room by proving track record early, showing a genuine point of view on his target markets, and being crystal clear that this is project-scoped work. Low-pressure, high-credibility. He ended the thread warmly ("I hope we can run a good business together and enjoy it 🤗") — the intent is there; this call is about earning the confidence to scope Phase 1.
